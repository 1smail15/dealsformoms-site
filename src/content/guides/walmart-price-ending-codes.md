That yellow tag above — **$1.37**, on a rack of kids' pajama sets — is a real
one I pulled off a Walmart clearance endcap. If you've ever wondered why a
clearance price lands on an oddly specific number like that instead of a
round $1.50 or $2.00, the cents digits are the most useful signal on the
shelf.

## The short version

Walmart's system marks items down in tiers, and the cents digits are how
staff track where an item sits in that sequence. Two endings matter most:

- **Prices ending in `.00`** — a rollback or a planned promotional price. It
  can still fall further.
- **Prices ending in `.03` (or, like that pajama tag, an odd number like
  `.37`)** — in most stores this signals a final or near-final markdown.
  What you see is close to the floor; the next step is usually the item
  leaving the shelf entirely, not a rounder price later.

## Why this is worth knowing

The difference decides whether you buy now or wait a week. A `.00` tag on
seasonal stock in early September will usually be cheaper by the end of the
month. An odd-cents tag like `$1.37` won't — and it's far more likely to be
gone if you come back for it.

> One caveat worth stating plainly: this is a convention, not a published
> policy, and it varies by store and by district. Treat it as a strong
> hint, not a rule.

## What to do at the shelf

1. Check the cents digits before anything else.
2. Scan the item in the Walmart app — the shelf tag is often behind the
   system price.
3. If it's an odd-cents ending like `.37` and the app price matches or is
   lower, buy it — that rack isn't getting restocked at that price.
